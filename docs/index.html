Persistwise on the web has closed. Thank you.

Persistwise set out to keep your writing safe even if the platform it was published on goes away. The website watched your feeds and kept each new post on the Wayback Machine, on IPFS and in a copy of its own.

We have decided to close the website. To everyone who signed up, tried it, sent a note or told a friend: thank you. You helped shape what comes next.

What happens now

  • The website has closed.
  • Wayback Machine snapshots are public and kept by the Internet Archive, so they stay where they are.

Hello, Persistwise Desktop

The same idea, now on your own computer: a personal web archive with no account and no Persistwise servers.

  • Save any page. Paste a link and Persistwise keeps a full copy (text, styles, images, icons and fonts) in one file that opens offline.
  • Watch your feeds. Follow a blog, newsletter or social feed (RSS, Atom or JSON Feed). Every new post is saved automatically.
  • Browse and search everything you saved, offline.
  • Wayback Machine. Pages are also sent to the Internet Archive's Save Page Now (on by default, easy to turn off).
  • IPFS, if you want it. Optional pinning to your own IPFS node or with your own Pinata key. Off by default.
  • Your data stays with you. Everything lives in a folder on your computer. No telemetry.

Persistwise Desktop is free on Linux. A Windows version through the Microsoft Store is planned.

Bring your web data over

Moving is a one-time step. Your feeds, posts, Wayback and IPFS links and saved copies come over, and the desktop app carries on checking your feeds.

  1. If you had an account, email help@ninepointlabs.com and we'll send your export.
  2. Import it. In Persistwise Desktop choose Import from Persistwise web and pick the zip, or run: 
    persistwise-desktop --import persistwise-export.zip
  3. That's it. Importing a fresher export again later updates what changed, with no duplicates. The website stored pages without their images, so the desktop app fetches and embeds them over the next few feed checks.
